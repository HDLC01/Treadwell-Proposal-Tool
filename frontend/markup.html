Kyle's markup rates, per sheet tab. Super & PTO and Soft Costs price the real bid; the other lines are stored and not read yet, and every row says which of the two it is.

How the chain works

The tabs are the five sheet layouts a bid can sit on — not work types. There is no Combo: a combo job is two option lines, each priced off its own tab.

The rows run in the order the chain compounds, and each line's base is the running total of everything above it — the small figure under each amount. A rate is applied to that base; GP and a typed dollar figure return dollars instead.

What reaches the bid. Super & PTO and Soft Costs are written into the estimate workbook's own rate cell, so Kyle's formulas work the dollars out from them on every tab of that layout and on every copied tab. A rate has to be a plain percent — 2.7% — to reach the workbook.

What does not, and why. GP is a tier ladder rather than a rate, the hard-bid discount reads a Yes/No box the tool cannot set on most tabs, on the gypsum tab Soft Costs is one of Kyle's own expressions, and Bond is held back because Kyle's bond row counts the two tax lines twice — any rate filed against it would over-charge until the sheet is corrected.

When a filed rate is read. An estimate screen reads these rates as it loads. One somebody already has open keeps the old rate until they reload, and reopening an older estimate reprices it at the new rate; what has already been sent to a customer stays as it was sent. The Polish beta is separate again — it still prices off the constants built into its own engine.

You can see these rates and how they price a job. Changing them is admin-only - ask Hanz or Kyle.
